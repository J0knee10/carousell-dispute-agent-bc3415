# Cost-based escalation threshold

_Generated by `uv run dispute-agent review-cost` (src/dispute_agent/evaluation/review_cost.py). Inputs: `review_cost` in config.yaml._

A person checks every recommendation. A confident one gets a standard review, which the agent makes faster; an escalated one gets a careful review, in which staff decide the dispute from scratch. Escalating more costs staff time; escalating less lets more wrong recommendations through. This report prices both and finds the confidence threshold with the lowest cost per dispute.

The rule is a cost-sensitive reject option (Chow, 1970): an answer deserves a careful review when the expected cost of letting it stand is more than the extra cost of the careful review. Gemini's confidence is not a probability, so the chance of a mistake at each confidence is measured on labelled cases, not read off the model.

## Answer

- **Chosen on dev cases:** at base costs the cheapest threshold is **0.95**. At the current 0.70 a dispute costs S$18.61; at 0.95 it costs S$18.31, S$0.30 less (2%), or about S$1,800 a year at 6,000 disputes.
- **Low and high inputs:** the cheapest threshold is 0.95 with every input at its low value and 0.70 with every input at its high value.
- **How sure:** in 2,000 resamples of the dev cases, a threshold above 0.70 is strictly cheaper than 0.70 in 68% of them and one below it in 0%.
- **Check on the test set (`cmp_gemini-flash-lite`, not used to choose):** 0.70 escalates 3 of 50 and catches 2 of 5 mistakes, at S$17.89 a dispute; 0.95 escalates 17, catches 3, at S$19.44. The cheapest on the test set would be 0.90.
- **AI usage** is about S$0.0032 per judged case (measured tokens at the paid-tier price), against S$13.12 of extra staff time for one escalation. It is the same at every threshold, so it does not move the choice.

A mistake let through costs one careful review of rework (the wronged side appeals) plus the money moved wrongly: the gap between the refund given and the refund due, as a share of the order.

**When escalating pays.** Escalating an answer costs the extra review for certain and saves the mistake cost only if the answer was wrong. So it pays when the chance of a mistake is above extra cost ÷ mistake cost:

## Caveats

- **Few mistakes.** The dev cases hold 4 mistakes, so one case more or less can move the answer. The resampling line above shows how much.
- **Dev labels** are the outcomes the cases were written for, not hand labels. The dev runs are from 28 Oct, before the `search_policy` tool was added; `gemini_seed` ran before the decision-guide fix. `dev_borderline_v2` holds only borderline cases, where mistakes are most common.
- **Two simplifications favour escalating:** a careful review is assumed always to reach the right answer, and a standard review to miss every mistake. If staff catch some mistakes in a standard review, escalating is worth less than shown.
- **The mistake cost** assumes every wrong decision is appealed and counts a full refund as lost even when the item goes back to the seller (both overstate it). It leaves out a user who stops using 'Buy' after an unfair decision (understates it).
- **The test set** has now been used for this check too; it played no part in choosing the threshold.
